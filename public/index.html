The short version

How I got here

I learned this work before it had a title. When English is your second language, being the person who makes two sides understand each other isn’t a career choice. It’s the job in the room.

Translating for patients who couldn’t ask their own doctor a question. Training law students to make an argument land in front of a judge. Coaching teams through agile transformations that actually stuck.

The setting kept changing. The problem never did — two people, both making perfect sense to themselves, neither one landing.

That has been the work for fifteen years. Most recently on the program that put generative AI aboard the International Space Station, the first large language model deployed in space, where the hard part was never the model. And for six years alongside it, volunteering as a mediator, sitting with people who had stopped speaking to each other altogether.

Different rooms. Same job.

Find where people are talking past each other, and close that gap. I believe collaboration and communication solve almost everything — at work, at home, on a tennis or pickleball court. And I believe you get there faster with clarity than with force. It’s the shortest route I’ve found.

Most people and most teams are one conversation away from the outcome they want.

Charisma you can practice in seven to nine minutes a day. A team that stops working around each other and starts performing. A business built on AI its leadership actually understands.

That’s the bridge I build. That’s why I’m here.

The method everything runs on

State · Story · Strategy

Three stages, in order — and the order is the method. You can’t sort out what happened while your body is still braced for a fight, and you can’t choose a strategy until you know which parts of the story you actually agree on.

First — State

Get your body out of threat before you open your mouth

Breath check. Jaw check. Rate the emotional charge. A regulated nervous system isn’t a nicety here — it is what makes the next two stages possible at all.

Then — Story

Separate what happened from what you made it mean

Two people, two accounts, no villain. Name the facts you both agree on before you touch the interpretation.

Finally — Strategy

Pick the one outcome you actually need

Not a script. A single defined ask, an opening line, and a decision about what you’ll do if the answer is no.

Privacy & security

What this site knows about you

Short version: if you only read, nothing about you is stored at all. If you sign the guestbook, three things are — your email, the name you choose to display, and a scrambled form of your password. You can download all of it or delete all of it from your account panel, any time, without asking me.

What is collected, and why

WhatWhyHow long
Email address To sign you in and identify your account. Never displayed to anyone else, never sold, never mailed to. Until you delete your account
Display name Shown publicly next to your notes, because a guestbook with anonymous notes isn’t a guestbook. Until you delete your account
Password Not stored. What is stored is a scrypt hash — a one-way scramble that can check your password without revealing it. Until you delete your account
Your notes and reactions They are the point of the guestbook. Notes are public; reactions show as counts. Until you delete them or your account
Session record To keep you signed in. The browser cookie holds a random token; the database holds only a hash of it. 30 days, or until you sign out
Security log Sign-ins, failed sign-ins and account changes, so a break-in attempt can be spotted and stopped. 30 days, then deleted automatically

What is deliberately not collected

  • No analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels, no fingerprinting.
  • No IP address is ever stored against a note. IP addresses appear only in the security log, and only as a keyed hash whose salt changes every day — enough to notice a burst of failed sign-ins, not enough to trace anyone.
  • No third-party embeds that could watch you. The only outside request the page makes is to Google Fonts for two typefaces.
  • No data is sold, rented, or shared with anyone. There is no one to share it with.

Cookies

Two, both strictly necessary, both set only after you sign in: one holds your session token, one holds a token that proves a form submission came from this page and not from somewhere else. Neither tracks you, neither follows you off this site, and there is no consent banner because there is nothing here that requires one.

What you can do, without asking

  • See everything. One button in your account panel downloads a JSON file of every row this site holds about you, including the security log.
  • Correct it. Change your display name or password in the same panel.
  • Delete it. One button. Your account, sessions and reactions go immediately. You choose whether your notes go with them or stay with your name stripped off.
  • Sign out everywhere. Ends every session on every device at once.

How it is protected

  • Passwords hashed with scrypt and a per-account random salt.
  • Session tokens stored only as hashes, so a stolen copy of the database hands over no live sessions.
  • Sign-in throttled per account and per source, and worded so it can’t be used to discover who has an account here.
  • A strict Content Security Policy that permits only this page’s own code, so an injected script has nowhere to run.
  • Every note is rendered as text, never as markup. Nothing anyone writes can execute.

Where things stand legally

Being straight with you about scope: this is a personal portfolio, and the privacy statutes people usually name — California’s CCPA/CPRA and the Tennessee Information Protection Act — set revenue and volume thresholds far above anything a site like this reaches, so neither currently binds it. The controls above exist because they are the right way to hold someone else’s data, not because a regulator is asking. If that changes, the mechanisms are already here.
